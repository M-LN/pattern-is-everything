Regression to the mean

Extreme results tend to be followed by less extreme ones. It looks like a force pulling things back; it is arithmetic. Watch the same idea turn into trading signals, portfolio rules and model penalties.

Start the trail →
  1. 1 Pattern Essays Regression to the Mean The core idea: an extreme outcome is partly luck, and luck does not repeat on demand — so the next result lands closer to the middle.
  2. 2 Market Psychology Mean Reversion Psychology Traders feel it as "what goes up must come down" — and easily mistake arithmetic for a promise.
  3. 3 Technical Indicators Relative Strength Index RSI turns the idea into a signal: overbought and oversold readings are bets on a move back toward the middle.
  4. 4 Risk & Portfolio Rebalancing Strategies Rebalancing leans on it by design, trimming what ran ahead and topping up what fell behind.
  5. 5 Timeseries Engineering Stationarity It only works if there is a stable mean to return to. Stationarity is the check that one exists.
  6. 6 ML Math Regularization Models get the same pull on purpose: regularization shrinks extreme weights toward zero so noise can't stretch them.
The pattern

Regression to the mean is a property of noisy measurement, not a law of markets. Where the mean itself moves, betting on the return is betting on the wrong thing.
