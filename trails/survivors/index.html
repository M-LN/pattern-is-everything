Survivors and false patterns

The data you can see is often the data that survived. Patterns found in it can be an artefact of what went missing. These stops show how the trap works, and the habits that keep you out of it.

Start the trail →
  1. 1 Pattern Essays Survivorship Bias We study what survived and quietly forget everything that did not — and the survivors look smarter than they were.
  2. 2 The Toolkit Survivorship & Look-Ahead Bias The same trap in a backtest: today's index members, or knowledge you would not have had at the time, make a strategy look better than it could have been.
  3. 3 Market Psychology Hindsight Bias After the fact, the outcome looks obvious. That feeling is the memory of what happened, not evidence you could have known.
  4. 4 The Toolkit Cross-Validation Done Right Honest evaluation starts with honest splits — no answers leaking into the data the model learns from.
  5. 5 Timeseries Engineering Backtesting Forecasts For anything ordered in time, test the way you would have used it: forward, one step at a time.
  6. 6 The Toolkit Learning Curves & Overfitting The gap between training and validation performance is overfitting made visible.
  7. 7 Pattern Essays The Map and the Territory Every model is a simplification. The danger is forgetting what was left out — including everything that did not survive into the data.
The pattern

Ask what is missing from the data, and what you know now that you would not have known then. Most false patterns live in one of those two gaps.
