Momentum

Recent direction tends to carry on, for a while. The same idea speeds up neural-network training, drives a family of trading indicators, shapes how traders add to winners and fuels the crowd. Follow it, and see where it stops paying.

Start the trail →
  1. 1 ML Math Optimizers Momentum in training: keep part of the last step's direction, so the optimiser rolls through small bumps instead of stalling at each one.
  2. 2 Technical Indicators Exponential Moving Average The same running memory applied to prices: an exponential average weights recent moves most, so it turns with the trend sooner than a simple average.
  3. 3 Technical Indicators MACD The gap between a fast and a slow average measures momentum directly. It widens when a move accelerates and narrows as it fades.
  4. 4 Technical Indicators Rate of Change Momentum at its plainest: the percentage change over N bars. A rising rate of change says the move is speeding up.
  5. 5 Risk & Portfolio Pyramiding Traders act on momentum by adding to positions that are working, in smaller tiers as the trend gets older.
  6. 6 Market Psychology FOMO The crowd's version: rising prices pull in buyers who fear missing out, and their buying carries the move further, until it stops.
  7. 7 Pattern Essays The Random Walk The sceptic's stop. If prices were a pure random walk, past direction would say nothing about the next step. Momentum strategies bet that they are not, and the evidence is mixed.
The pattern

Momentum is memory: of the gradient, of the price, of the crowd. It helps while the direction holds and hurts at the turn, which is why every version comes with a way to let go.
